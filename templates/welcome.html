How it works

  1. Describe a versionPick a project and give the repository address, commit, tech stack and what you want looked at. No passwords or tokens are ever needed.
  2. Choose reviewersPick one to five reviewers from the directory and decide how many reports you need.
  3. Read the reportsEach report states what was and was not assessed, lists strengths, and gives findings with severity, explanation and a suggested fix.
  4. Fix and verifyMove each finding from open to in progress to fixed. Only the original reviewer can mark it resolved.
  5. Follow up or shareStart a new version for another round, and see progress across versions. Sharing a version is optional and revocable.

Two review goals

Production readiness

Reviewers look for security, correctness, reliability, performance, testing and deployment gaps, and say what they found and what they did not check.

Interview preparation

Reviewers look at how well you could explain and defend the project, point out gaps in depth, and suggest questions an interviewer might ask.

What a review is, and is not

A review is a written opinion from an individual community reviewer about one commit. It is not a certification, an endorsement or a guarantee, and a completed review does not mean a project is ready. Reviewers’ skills, experience and links are self-reported and Revora has not verified any of them. Separate accounts do not prove that reviewers are independent of the author, and Revora cannot tell whether one person controls several accounts.

FAQ

Who can be a reviewer?

Anyone can create a reviewer account and profile. Everything on a profile is self-reported; the platform verifies none of it. Check a reviewer’s links before choosing them.

Does “review complete” mean my project is ready?

No. It only records that the number of reports you asked for has been submitted. Read the findings, including critical and unresolved ones.

Who can see my reviews?

Only you and the reviewers you assigned, until you create a public link for one version. A shared page shows that version’s non-private report text and all of its findings. It never shows reviewers’ private notes, e-mail addresses, your questions or your other projects. You can revoke a link at any time and sharing again creates a new link.

What do reviewers agree to?

Before a reviewer submits a report they must tick a statement that the author may publish the report’s non-private text and findings if the author chooses to share it. A report cannot be submitted without it.

Can I review my own project?

No. The same account can never review its own work. Someone who controls two accounts could still try to, and Revora cannot detect that.

What about the earlier verified proofs?

They stay exactly as they were, including their history and public links.

Is the record tamper-proof?

No, it is tamper-evident. Anyone with database access could rewrite it and its hashes together; the check detects ordinary edits and inconsistencies.
